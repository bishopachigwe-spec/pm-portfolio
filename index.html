Case Study 01

M36: Reviving a declining digital investment platform

TurnaroundStrategyGrowthCX
Context

M36 had declining onboarding and adoption, production issues and limited product visibility. The platform had capacity, but the internal organisation and customer-facing strategy were not translating that capacity into sustained adoption.

Core strategy

Internal adoption to external scale.

Build understanding and confidence inside the bank first, turn staff into informed advocates, then scale the proposition externally to customers.

What I led
1

Stabilise the fundamentals. Identified production issues and led efforts to restore non-functional services, including SMS OTP capabilities important to onboarding, password recovery and device changes.

2

Set a clear direction. Developed a strategy document, aligned the M36 team, and used multiple internal presentations to create shared understanding of the plan.

3

Build stakeholder buy-in. Engaged teams including Corporate Communications and Treasury to clarify the support required for execution.

4

Enable the organisation. Led bank-wide, frontline and departmental training so staff understood the product, could confidently use it and could advocate for it.

5

Close the feedback loop. Used customer feedback and analytics to shape priorities, monitor the pulse of the product and adjust decisions after deployment.

6

Define the next product. Began shaping M36 V2 as a more accessible financial lifestyle ecosystem rather than a product limited to investment transactions.

“The product had capacity. The bigger problem was visibility, confidence and execution.”
Reported impact

The onboarding decline was reversed, reactivation and onboarding improved materially, and reporting became consistently positive month on month. Team morale also improved as the team began to see tangible results from the strategy.

Case Study 02

UnionMobile 2.0: Rebuilding digital banking around self-service

Product StrategyUXSelf-ServiceRisk
Problem

The previous application was developed and managed by an external vendor. The bank shared revenue with the vendor, feature delivery and support were slowed by the dependency, and maintenance was costly.

The experience also relied heavily on assisted service for routine requests that could potentially be completed by customers themselves.

Product vision

Give customers more control, without weakening the bank's controls.

Move routine activities into self-service, shorten unnecessary journeys and create an internally owned product that the bank can evolve more directly.

Research inputs

Customer complaints data, contact-centre data, app reviews, customer interviews, surveys, usability testing, transaction data, journey analysis, branch feedback and competitor and industry analysis.

Key product decisions
  • Self-service PIN change, password change and reset, security-word creation and reset.
  • Self-service account opening and account upgrades.
  • Self-service transaction-limit increases up to ₦25m, compared with the previous ₦5m self-service ceiling.
  • Profile and device unblock journeys to reduce dependence on email, contact centre, chat and branch assistance.
  • Alternative authentication and verification options, including security questions, biometrics, liveness checks and Union Token as a soft-token option.
  • Shorter journeys and removal of redundant or repeated processes.
The hard part

The shift from assisted service to greater self-service created legitimate concerns across Compliance, IT Control and Information Security. I pushed for the customer experience improvements while working with those teams to understand the policy and control requirements and design mitigations rather than treating risk as a reason to stop product progress.

“The question became: how can we let the customer do this themselves safely?”
My contribution

I worked with the project team, developers, designers, Service Assurance, Compliance, IT Control, InfoSec, Corporate Communications and executive stakeholders. I contributed to the BRS, documented and reviewed screen copy, co-designed the UI, created user stories and reviewed requirements, policies and features as development progressed.

Current status

The application has been deployed to the Apple App Store and Google Play Store and accepted by both platforms. Customer deployment and migration are pending Executive Committee approval.

Because customer migration has not yet happened, post-launch adoption, service-volume, satisfaction and financial impact metrics are intentionally not presented as achieved outcomes.
