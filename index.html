Case study 01

M36: Turning around a declining digital investment platform

TurnaroundStrategyGrowthCX
Context

M36 was experiencing declining performance, production issues and weak visibility. The platform had capacity, but the organisation was not translating that capacity into sustained adoption.

Core strategy

Internal adoption → external scale.

Build understanding and confidence inside the bank first, turn staff into informed advocates, then scale the proposition externally.

What I led
1

Stabilised the fundamentals. Identified production issues and led restoration of non-functional services, including SMS OTP capabilities supporting onboarding, password recovery and device changes.

2

Created the strategy. Developed the strategy document, aligned the team through multiple internal presentations, and established a shared operating direction.

3

Secured stakeholder buy-in. Engaged Corporate Communications, Treasury and other stakeholders around the support required to execute the strategy.

4

Enabled the organisation. Led bank-wide, frontline and departmental training so staff understood the product, could confidently use it and could advocate for it.

5

Closed the feedback loop. Used customer feedback and analytics to prioritise improvements and continuously feel the pulse of the product.

6

Defined the next horizon. Began shaping M36 V2 as a broader financial lifestyle ecosystem rather than an investment-only experience.

The product had capacity. The bigger problem was visibility, confidence and execution.
Strategy

Stabilise → align → educate → drive internal adoption → turn staff into advocates → scale externally.

Reported impact

The declining onboarding trend reversed, onboarding and reactivation improved materially, reporting became consistently positive month on month, and team morale improved as the team saw tangible results from the strategy.

Case study 02

UnionMobile 2.0: Rebuilding digital banking around self-service

Product StrategyUXResearchRisk
Problem

The previous application was developed and managed by an external vendor. The bank shared revenue with the vendor, while feature delivery and support were slowed by dependency and bureaucracy. The experience also relied heavily on assisted service for routine requests.

Product vision

Give customers more control without weakening the bank's controls.

Build an internally owned product, increase self-service, shorten journeys and remove unnecessary friction.

Research input

Customer complaints data, contact-centre data, app reviews, customer interviews, surveys, usability testing, transaction data, journey analysis, branch feedback and competitor and industry analysis.

Product vision

A more user-friendly, self-service-driven mobile banking experience where customers can safely resolve routine needs without unnecessary contact-centre, chat or branch intervention.

Key product decisions
  • PIN change, password change and reset, security-word creation and reset.
  • Self-service account opening and upgrades, including progression to Tier 3.
  • Self-service transaction-limit increases up to ₦25m, versus the previous ₦5m ceiling.
  • Self-service device/profile unblock journeys.
  • Security questions, biometrics, liveness verification and Union Token as alternative authentication/verification mechanisms.
  • Shorter transaction journeys and elimination of redundant or repeated processes.
The hard part

The move from assisted service to greater self-service created legitimate pushback from Compliance, IT Control and Information Security. I pushed the customer experience objective while working through policy interpretation, risk and control requirements to find a safe landing.

The question became: how can we let the customer do this themselves safely?
My contribution

I worked across the project team, developers, designers, Service Assurance, Compliance, IT Control, InfoSec, Corporate Communications and executive stakeholders. I contributed to the BRS, documented and reviewed screen copy, co-designed the UI, created user stories and reviewed requirements, policies and features through development.

How I think about products

Principles shaped by operating experience across customers, technology, risk, commercial teams and frontline operations.

Start with the problem.

Customer pain, business constraints and evidence come before the feature.

Make complexity invisible.

The underlying product can be complex. The customer's journey should not have to be.

Design for the real world.

Products must work within how customers actually behave and how organisations actually operate.

Build for resilience.

Dependencies, failure modes, operational realities and recovery paths are product concerns too.

Think commercially.

Customer value and business value should reinforce each other.

Align people before scaling.

A strategy only becomes a product when the people around it understand it, support it and execute it.
